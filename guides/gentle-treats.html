Polite mouth

Teach gentle treat taking

Teach your dog to take food softly by rewarding a pause, using a flat palm, and ending the hand game when teeth make contact.

Best forFoundations
Session2–4 min
LevelEasy

Goal

What success looks like

Your dog takes a treat from your open palm with a soft mouth and no painful grabbing.

Visual method

See the sequence before you practice

A soft mouth is taught through a calm pause and a flat palm. Close the hand if teeth make contact, then try again.

Complete method

One session, start to finish

  1. 01

    Set up

    Use soft pea-sized treats that can be eaten quickly.

  2. 02

    Warm up

    Repeat one skill your dog already knows for 30 to 60 seconds.

  3. 03

    Teach

    Hold a treat in a closed fist and wait until your dog stops nudging, licking, or mouthing the hand. Mark and reward the pause.

  4. 04

    Reward

    Mark the exact behavior, then deliver the reward within one second.

  5. 05

    Repeat

    Do three to five easy repetitions. If two cues are missed, make the task easier.

  6. 06

    Advance

    Your dog takes a treat from a flat palm softly in eight of ten easy repetitions, without grabbing or making painful contact.

  7. 07

    Finish

    Use your release cue, reward one easy win, and end while your dog is still engaged.

Before you begin

Set up for an easy win

  • Use soft pea-sized treats that can be eaten quickly.
  • Start with a calm dog and a quiet room.
  • Use an open palm rather than offering a treat between fingers.
  • Keep sessions very short if your dog gets excited around food.

Step by step

How to teach it

  1. 01

    Start with a closed hand

    Hold a treat in a closed fist and wait until your dog stops nudging, licking, or mouthing the hand. Mark and reward the pause.

  2. 02

    Open the palm slowly

    Open your hand flat with the treat in the center. If teeth make contact, close the hand and pause. Try again when the dog is calm.

  3. 03

    Reward the soft take

    When the dog takes the treat gently, mark, praise calmly, and allow the food to be eaten. Keep your hand still.

  4. 04

    Add the word Gentle

    Say “Gentle” once before offering the treat. Use a quiet voice and reward the soft mouth rather than repeating the cue.

  5. 05

    Practice with different people

    Have another calm adult practice with the same hand position and cue. Avoid practicing with children without direct supervision.

  6. 06

    Use the skill in daily life

    Practice before walks, meals, and greetings. If your dog becomes too excited, return to a closed hand and make the session easier.

✓

Success checkpoint

You are ready to add difficulty when…

Your dog takes a treat from a flat palm softly in eight of ten easy repetitions, without grabbing or making painful contact.

When progress stalls

Common problems and fixes

My dog snaps at the treat.

Use a closed hand and reward only after the dog pauses. Ask a qualified trainer for help if snapping is frequent or feels unsafe.

My dog ignores the treat.

Try a higher-value food or practice when the dog is not full. Keep the session to three or four repetitions.

My dog gets more excited when I say Gentle.

Do not repeat the cue. Return to a closed fist, lower your energy, and reward the pause before adding the spoken cue again.

Trainer note

Gentle is not about suppressing excitement. It teaches a clear, comfortable way to receive food without forcing the dog to guess.
