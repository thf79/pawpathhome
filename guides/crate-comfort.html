Safe resting space

Build crate comfort

Create a crate that the dog chooses for rest, with gradual door practice and no punishment.

Best forPuppy
Session3–8 min
LevelGradual

Goal

What success looks like

Your dog enters the crate willingly and stays calm with the door open or closed for short periods.

Visual method

See the sequence before you practice

The crate should become a voluntary rest area. Build entry, calm behavior, and door duration in separate small steps.

Complete method

One session, start to finish

  1. 01

    Set up

    Choose a crate large enough for the dog to stand, turn, and lie down comfortably.

  2. 02

    Warm up

    Repeat one skill your dog already knows for 30 to 60 seconds.

  3. 03

    Teach

    Toss a treat inside the crate and let the dog walk in and out freely. Do not close the door. Repeat until entering is easy and voluntary.

  4. 04

    Reward

    Mark the exact behavior, then deliver the reward within one second.

  5. 05

    Repeat

    Do three to five easy repetitions. If two cues are missed, make the task easier.

  6. 06

    Advance

    Your dog enters the crate willingly and remains calm with the door closed for several minutes, without panic, pacing, or distress.

  7. 07

    Finish

    Use your release cue, reward one easy win, and end while your dog is still engaged.

Before you begin

Set up for an easy win

  • Choose a crate large enough for the dog to stand, turn, and lie down comfortably.
  • Remove the door or secure it open during the first sessions.
  • Use high-value food, a favorite chew, or a stuffed food toy.
  • Place the crate in a quiet area where the dog can still see the household.

Step by step

How to teach it

  1. 01

    Build a positive association

    Toss a treat inside the crate and let the dog walk in and out freely. Do not close the door. Repeat until entering is easy and voluntary.

  2. 02

    Reward calm inside

    When your dog steps inside, mark and reward calmly near the back of the crate. Let the dog choose when to leave.

  3. 03

    Feed near and inside

    Move meals or food toys near the crate, then inside with the door open. Keep the experience quiet and pressure-free.

  4. 04

    Close for one second

    With the dog comfortable inside, close the door for one second, mark, reward, and open it before the dog becomes worried.

  5. 05

    Build duration slowly

    Add a few seconds at a time, then step one foot away. Return, reward calm behavior, and open the door before distress begins.

  6. 06

    Practice real-life rest

    Use the crate for short rests while you are nearby, then build to brief separations. Always provide water, comfort, and an appropriate duration for the dog’s age and needs.

✓

Success checkpoint

You are ready to add difficulty when…

Your dog enters the crate willingly and remains calm with the door closed for several minutes, without panic, pacing, or distress.

When progress stalls

Common problems and fixes

My dog only enters for food.

That is a normal starting point. Keep meals and chews in the crate, reward calm behavior, and do not rush the door.

My dog panics when the door closes.

Open the door immediately and go back to easier separation practice. Contact a qualified force-free behavior professional if distress repeats.

My dog is fine at night but not during the day.

Practice short daytime rests with the same routine. Keep the crate comfortable and avoid using it only when you leave.

Trainer note

A crate should be a resting option, not a punishment room. Never force a frightened dog inside or use the crate to suppress barking or fear.

Safety first
