Door safety

Teach Wait at doors

Build a pause at closed doors and teach the dog to wait for a clear release before moving through.

Best forLife skills
Session3–5 min
LevelModerate

Goal

What success looks like

Your dog pauses while a door opens and moves through only after the release cue.

Visual method

See the sequence before you practice

Wait means pause until released. Keep the door opening small enough that the dog can succeed.

Complete method

One session, start to finish

  1. 01

    Set up

    Choose one low-traffic door in the home and keep a leash on the dog at first.

  2. 02

    Warm up

    Repeat one skill your dog already knows for 30 to 60 seconds.

  3. 03

    Teach

    Stand near the closed door, wait for your dog to stop pulling, then mark and reward. Repeat until standing calmly is easy.

  4. 04

    Reward

    Mark the exact behavior, then deliver the reward within one second.

  5. 05

    Repeat

    Do three to five easy repetitions. If two cues are missed, make the task easier.

  6. 06

    Advance

    Your dog pauses while you open a quiet door and waits for the release cue in eight of ten easy repetitions.

  7. 07

    Finish

    Use your release cue, reward one easy win, and end while your dog is still engaged.

Before you begin

Set up for an easy win

  • Choose one low-traffic door in the home and keep a leash on the dog at first.
  • Use a clear release cue such as “Free” or “Okay.”
  • Prepare treats that can be delivered from your hand or tossed safely.
  • Never test the skill near traffic or an unfenced exit.

Step by step

How to teach it

  1. 01

    Reward a pause at the closed door

    Stand near the closed door, wait for your dog to stop pulling, then mark and reward. Repeat until standing calmly is easy.

  2. 02

    Touch the door

    Put your hand on the handle and reward if your dog stays in place. If your dog moves forward, remove your hand and wait for the pause again.

  3. 03

    Open one inch

    Say “Wait” once, open the door a tiny amount, and close it calmly if your dog moves toward the gap. Reward any pause before the doorway.

  4. 04

    Build the opening

    Open the door farther in small steps, always returning to an easier opening if the dog moves. Reward while the dog is still waiting.

  5. 05

    Add the release cue

    Say “Free” and invite your dog through only after a successful wait. Reward on the other side of the doorway.

  6. 06

    Practice different doors

    Repeat in another room, then at a quiet exterior door while on leash. Keep a hand on the door and never rely on Wait alone for safety.

✓

Success checkpoint

You are ready to add difficulty when…

Your dog pauses while you open a quiet door and waits for the release cue in eight of ten easy repetitions.

When progress stalls

Common problems and fixes

My dog rushes through the gap.

Make the opening smaller, use a leash and barrier, and reward before the dog reaches the doorway. Do not close the door on the dog.

My dog cannot hold still at the door.

Practice with the door closed or use a baby gate at a safe distance. Reward calm standing before adding any movement.

My dog only waits for treats.

Keep the reward behind your back, reward after the pause, and vary food with praise or a chance to go through.

Trainer note

Wait is a safety skill, but management still matters. Doors, gates, and leashes should remain secure around traffic and unknown dogs.
