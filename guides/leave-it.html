Impulse control

Teach leave it with less frustration

Teach a pause and a check-in before your dog has a chance to grab the item.

Best forFoundations
Session3–5 min
LevelEasy

Goal

What success looks like

Your dog pauses and looks toward you when you say “Leave it” around a low-value item.

Visual method

See the sequence before you practice

Leave it teaches a pause and a check-in. The dog should turn away from the item before receiving a reward from another hand.

Complete method

One session, start to finish

  1. 01

    Set up

    Use two kinds of treats: a low-value treat in view and a higher-value reward hidden in your pouch.

  2. 02

    Warm up

    Repeat one skill your dog already knows for 30 to 60 seconds.

  3. 03

    Teach

    Place a low-value treat in a closed hand and wait. When your dog stops trying to pry it open and looks away or at you, mark and reward with the other hand.

  4. 04

    Reward

    Mark the exact behavior, then deliver the reward within one second.

  5. 05

    Repeat

    Do three to five easy repetitions. If two cues are missed, make the task easier.

  6. 06

    Advance

    Your dog disengages from a low-value item after one cue and looks toward you in eight of ten easy repetitions.

  7. 07

    Finish

    Use your release cue, reward one easy win, and end while your dog is still engaged.

Before you begin

Set up for an easy win

  • Use two kinds of treats: a low-value treat in view and a higher-value reward hidden in your pouch.
  • Start with an item that is safe and easy to remove.
  • Do not practice with dangerous, medical, or resource-guarded items.
  • Keep the item under your control so your dog cannot grab it.

Step by step

How to teach it

  1. 01

    Start with a closed hand

    Place a low-value treat in a closed hand and wait. When your dog stops trying to pry it open and looks away or at you, mark and reward with the other hand.

  2. 02

    Move the hand

    Open the hand briefly, close it if your dog moves toward it, and reward the pause or check-in. Keep the pace calm.

  3. 03

    Add the cue

    Say “Leave it” once just before presenting the closed hand. Reward from your other hand after your dog disengages.

  4. 04

    Practice with a treat on the floor

    Place a low-value treat on the floor and cover it with your shoe or hand if needed. Reward the moment your dog backs away or looks up.

  5. 05

    Add distance

    Move one step away from the covered item, give the cue, and return to reward. If your dog dives for it, reduce the difficulty.

  6. 06

    Generalize slowly

    Practice with different safe objects and rooms. Use management first when the real-world item is dangerous.

✓

Success checkpoint

You are ready to add difficulty when…

Your dog disengages from a low-value item after one cue and looks toward you in eight of ten easy repetitions.

When progress stalls

Common problems and fixes

My dog grabs the treat before I can cover it.

Use a lower-value treat, keep it under your hand, and reward any pause. Do not chase or pull the item from your dog’s mouth.

My dog only responds when I have a better treat.

That is a normal early stage. Keep the high-value reward hidden, then vary rewards once the behavior is reliable.

My dog guards objects.

Do not practice leave it with a guarded item. Contact a qualified force-free behavior professional and keep everyone safe.

Trainer note

Leave it is not a test of who is in charge. It is a practical cue for pausing and reconnecting before access.
