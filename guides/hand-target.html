Nose to palm

Teach a hand target

Teach your dog to touch their nose to your open palm, then use that simple action to guide and redirect them.

Best forFoundations
Session3–5 min
LevelEasy

Goal

What success looks like

Your dog touches their nose to your open palm after one Touch cue.

Visual method

See the sequence before you practice

The dog should move their nose to your palm, not the other way around. Keep your hand still and reward with the other hand.

Complete method

One session, start to finish

  1. 01

    Set up

    Choose one open-hand signal and use it consistently.

  2. 02

    Warm up

    Repeat one skill your dog already knows for 30 to 60 seconds.

  3. 03

    Teach

    Hold your palm near your dog’s nose and wait. Do not wave it or push toward the face. Most dogs will investigate the hand naturally.

  4. 04

    Reward

    Mark the exact behavior, then deliver the reward within one second.

  5. 05

    Repeat

    Do three to five easy repetitions. If two cues are missed, make the task easier.

  6. 06

    Advance

    Your dog touches the palm after one cue in eight of ten repetitions and can follow a target one step to the left or right.

  7. 07

    Finish

    Use your release cue, reward one easy win, and end while your dog is still engaged.

Before you begin

Set up for an easy win

  • Choose one open-hand signal and use it consistently.
  • Use soft treats your dog can eat quickly.
  • Start in a quiet room with space to move a few steps.
  • Keep your hand relaxed and avoid pushing it into the dog’s face.

Step by step

How to teach it

  1. 01

    Offer an open palm

    Hold your palm near your dog’s nose and wait. Do not wave it or push toward the face. Most dogs will investigate the hand naturally.

  2. 02

    Mark nose contact

    The instant the nose touches your palm, mark with “Yes” and reward from the other hand. Move the reward hand away from the target hand.

  3. 03

    Repeat five easy trials

    Reset between repetitions and present the palm in the same way. Mark contact, reward, and pause before the next try.

  4. 04

    Add the cue

    Once the touch is reliable, say “Touch” just before presenting your palm. Use the cue once and help only by making the target easier.

  5. 05

    Move the target

    Present your palm slightly left, right, higher, or lower. Reward the nose contact rather than the dog following the hand.

  6. 06

    Use targeting in real life

    Use Touch to move your dog off a path, onto a scale, or away from a doorway. Keep the action positive and never use it to pull the dog by the head.

✓

Success checkpoint

You are ready to add difficulty when…

Your dog touches the palm after one cue in eight of ten repetitions and can follow a target one step to the left or right.

When progress stalls

Common problems and fixes

My dog licks or paws my hand.

Reward only the nose touch at first. Keep your hand still and mark the exact moment the nose makes contact.

My dog backs away from my hand.

Start with the palm farther away, reduce eye contact, and reward any deliberate movement toward the hand. Do not reach toward the dog.

My dog targets every hand.

Use a clear cue and put your hands away when you are not training. Reward only after the spoken or visual cue.

Trainer note

A hand target is a useful conversation tool. It gives the dog a positive action to perform instead of simply being told to stop.
