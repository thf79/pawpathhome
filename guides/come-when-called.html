Recall

Build a stronger come-when-called

Make coming back predictably rewarding, then add distance and distraction slowly.

Best forLife skills
Session3–5 min
LevelModerate

Goal

What success looks like

Your dog turns, comes toward you, and allows a gentle collar touch or hand target indoors.

Visual method

See the sequence before you practice

The dog should move toward you, arrive close enough to touch the collar, and receive the reward after returning.

Complete method

One session, start to finish

  1. 01

    Set up

    Use a special high-value reward reserved for recall practice.

  2. 02

    Warm up

    Repeat one skill your dog already knows for 30 to 60 seconds.

  3. 03

    Teach

    Say your recall word, immediately mark, and feed a treat. Repeat five times without asking your dog to move. This builds a positive prediction.

  4. 04

    Reward

    Mark the exact behavior, then deliver the reward within one second.

  5. 05

    Repeat

    Do three to five easy repetitions. If two cues are missed, make the task easier.

  6. 06

    Advance

    Your dog comes toward you after one cue in nine of ten repetitions indoors, without you chasing, repeating, or showing a treat first.

  7. 07

    Finish

    Use your release cue, reward one easy win, and end while your dog is still engaged.

Before you begin

Set up for an easy win

  • Use a special high-value reward reserved for recall practice.
  • Start on a 4 to 6 foot leash indoors or in a safely enclosed space.
  • Avoid calling your dog only when something unpleasant will happen.
  • Plan to reward every successful return during the early stages.

Step by step

How to teach it

  1. 01

    Charge the recall word

    Say your recall word, immediately mark, and feed a treat. Repeat five times without asking your dog to move. This builds a positive prediction.

  2. 02

    Call from one step away

    Take one step away, say the recall word once, and reward generously when your dog reaches you. Then say your release cue.

  3. 03

    Add distance gradually

    Increase to three, then six, then ten feet. Use a happy voice and move backward only if your dog understands the game.

  4. 04

    Practice easy hide-and-seek

    Step behind a doorway or around a safe corner and call once. Reward the search and return, then make the next round easier.

  5. 05

    Add a collar touch

    When your dog arrives, gently touch their collar, mark, and reward. This makes leash handling easier and prevents hand shyness.

  6. 06

    Use a long line outdoors

    In a safely enclosed area, practice on a long line. Never rely on an untrained recall near traffic or other hazards.

✓

Success checkpoint

You are ready to add difficulty when…

Your dog comes toward you after one cue in nine of ten repetitions indoors, without you chasing, repeating, or showing a treat first.

When progress stalls

Common problems and fixes

My dog comes only when I show the treat.

Return to a shorter distance, say the cue once, and reward after arrival. Keep the reward hidden until after the response.

My dog comes close but then runs away.

Reward the arrival, briefly touch the collar, and release before your dog decides to leave. Practice the collar touch separately with easy rewards.

My dog ignores me outdoors.

That is normal at first. Use a long line, increase distance from distractions, and hire a qualified force-free trainer if safety is a concern.

Trainer note

Never punish a dog after they come to you. Even a mild scolding can make returning less likely next time.
